# Narration for the demo reel — one line per beat, spoken in order.
#
# Lines starting with # are comments, and so are the "# ACT n" markers: they carry the
# story structure without reaching the voice. A beat's footage is recorded by
# scripts/record-demo.mjs under the same id; scripts/build-demo-video.mjs synthesises
# each line and holds the footage for exactly as long as the line takes, so editing a
# line here re-times the cut without re-recording anything.
#
# The vocabulary law in hackathon/VOCABULARY.md is binding here too: no camouflage,
# flawless, glowing, perfect skin, flaws, simulated. "Glow" is avoided entirely — it is
# one inflection from a banned word and this is the artifact a grep cannot audit. The
# product still ships "glow check-in", so that panel is not filmed.
#
# Every number spoken here has a row in hackathon/CLAIM_PROOF_MAP.md. Two claims are
# deliberately NOT made, because review 003 proved them false on the run this reel plays
# over: that the colour read chooses the garment on the wedding path (one masculine cut
# exists in the wardrobe, so formality decides — deterministic.ts:369-374 weights
# undertone 2 against formality 4), and that all four renders carry a receipt (three of
# the four do). Both are said accurately in b10 and b12 instead of being left out.

# ACT 1 — Why this exists (target 0:00–0:24)
b01-why | Most virtual try-on demos stop at "here is a garment on you." But before a wedding I want three things answered in one place: what to do about my skin between now and the day, what to wear for my colouring, and where to buy all of it.
b02-promise | Every tool I tried answered one of the three. Aphrodite answers all three, from a single photo.

# ACT 2 — The loop (target 0:24–0:58)
b03-input | You give it the occasion and one selfie. It asks which cut you want rather than reading that off your face — with a wardrobe this unbalanced, a guess dressed up as a choice is worse than a question.
b04-run | Then you watch it work. Four YouCam API steps chained in one run — and in demo mode the whole board lands in about four and a half seconds.
b05-board | The board arrives whole: your skin read, your colours, a countdown to the day, the outfit rendered on you, and a basket that prices the lot.

# ACT 3 — The highlights, including the one thing it cannot do (target 0:58–1:52)
b06-skin | YouCam Skin Analysis scores ten concerns, and the plan leads with the lowest one — not with the flattering ones.
b07-colour | YouCam Skin-Tone reads the real colour of this face: undertone, depth, a palette.
b08-tryon | Apparel Try-On puts the Slate Blue three-piece on this exact photo, and Photo Lighting relights that same photo for the evening.
b09-countdown | The countdown changes in kind with the date. Three weeks out it front-loads actives; the night before it stops them and switches to hydration.
b10-second-face | Here is where the two halves actually meet. A second face reads cool, and the colour read has nine feminine cuts to choose among — so it picks a cool one, the Sky Wrap Maxi, rendered on her own photo.
b11-honest-limit | On the first face it cannot do that, and it does not fake it. One masculine cut exists in this whole wardrobe, so formality decides — and the copy just reads "right for a wedding", with no colour reasoning at all. Silence, where there is nothing true to say.

# ACT 4 — The self-catch (target 1:52–2:24)
b12-refuse | Now the part demos skip. Hand it a face it has never rendered and it refuses. Renders are keyed to the person they depict, because showing you a stranger's body is worse than showing you nothing.
b13-ledger | The ledger is as blunt: nothing was called just now, these are captured renders, and three of these four are byte-identical to a committed Perfect Corp receipt. Hash them yourself.
b14-verify | This endpoint replays the real task IDs behind those renders at zero cost — including the call that failed, and the one that rejected our own sample photo.

# ACT 5 — How it is built, and the close (target 2:24–2:50)
b15-engines | Two engines share one event stream. This run is the rule engine — it needs no model key at all. Unlocked, GPT orchestrates the same APIs as tools; and if it stalls, the rule engine finishes the run and says so out loud.
b16-close | A hundred and eighteen tests, and one of them fails if a render ever claims a receipt it cannot match. It is live, it is open, and it costs nothing to look. Aphrodite — Skin AI and try-on, in one occasion.
